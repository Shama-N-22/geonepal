WHY THIS EXISTS

Nepal lives with the ground moving beneath it.

Nepal sits along one of the most seismically active mountain belts on Earth, where the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates continue to collide beneath the Himalaya. The same steep terrain that makes the country's landscape extraordinary also makes it acutely vulnerable — to earthquakes, to monsoon flooding, and to the landslides that follow when saturated slopes give way. Thousands of smaller incidents are recorded here every year, most of them never reaching international attention.

Why we built GeoNepal

Data about these events exists — scattered across government portals, seismic catalogs, and international databases — but rarely in one place, and rarely in a form anyone outside a technical team can actually look at. GeoNepal exists to pull that real data together: live from Nepal's BIPAD disaster portal, the USGS earthquake catalog, and GDACS, presented as something a person can actually explore, incident by incident, year by year.
